## Budgeting with the 50-30-20 guideline
A simple guideline splits take-home income into about 50 percent needs (rent, groceries, EMIs, bills), 30 percent wants and 20 percent savings and investments. It is a starting point, not a rule: in expensive cities needs may take a larger share, and the savings share can be raised when EMIs end.

## Emergency fund
An emergency fund of 3 to 6 months of essential expenses, kept in a savings account, liquid fund or short fixed deposit, protects against job loss or medical bills. Build it before taking long-term investment risk, and keep it separate from daily spending money.

## What is a SIP
A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) invests a fixed amount in a mutual fund at regular intervals, usually monthly. It spreads purchases across market highs and lows (rupee-cost averaging) and builds discipline. A SIP is a way to invest, not a type of fund. Returns are not guaranteed and depend on the fund and the market.

## SIP future value formula
The future value of a monthly SIP can be estimated as FV = P x (((1+i)^n - 1) / i) x (1+i), where P is the monthly amount, i is the monthly rate of return (annual rate / 12 / 100) and n is the number of months. This is an illustration with an assumed return, not a forecast.

## Mutual fund basics
Mutual funds are regulated by SEBI. Equity funds invest mainly in shares and can swing widely in the short term; debt funds invest in bonds; hybrid funds mix both. Check the expense ratio, the fund's risk label (riskometer), the investment horizon and the fund house, and invest through SEBI-registered channels. Direct plans have lower expense ratios than regular plans.

## Insurance basics
Term life insurance gives a high cover for a low premium and is generally considered a protection product rather than an investment. Health insurance protects savings from large hospital bills. Compare cover, waiting periods and claim settlement record before buying.
