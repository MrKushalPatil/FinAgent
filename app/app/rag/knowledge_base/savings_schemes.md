## Public Provident Fund (PPF)
PPF is a 15-year government-backed scheme with a lock-in, partial withdrawals after the early years, and contributions from 500 up to 1.5 lakh rupees a year. Interest is set by the government every quarter and has recently been around 7.1 percent a year. Under the old tax regime, deposits qualify for Section 80C and the interest and maturity amount are tax-free. Rates change, so check the latest notification.

## Sukanya Samriddhi Yojana (SSY)
SSY is for the girl child: an account can be opened in the name of a girl below 10 years of age. Minimum deposit is 250 rupees and maximum is 1.5 lakh rupees a year. The account matures 21 years after opening and deposits are needed for the first 15 years. It has offered one of the higher small-savings rates, recently about 8.2 percent. Check the current rate.

## National Savings Certificate (NSC)
NSC is a 5-year post office certificate with a fixed rate declared at purchase and interest compounding annually. Under the old tax regime the investment qualifies for Section 80C. Interest is taxable but reinvested interest is treated as eligible for 80C in the first four years.

## Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS)
SCSS is for people aged 60 and above, with a 5-year term that can be extended, and quarterly interest payouts. The maximum investment limit was raised to 30 lakh rupees in 2023. The rate has recently been about 8.2 percent. Interest is taxable.

## Employees' Provident Fund (EPF)
Salaried employees in covered establishments contribute 12 percent of basic pay plus dearness allowance to EPF, and the employer contributes a matching share split between EPF and pension. The EPF interest rate is declared each year and has recently been in the range of 8.1 to 8.25 percent. Withdrawals have conditions; check EPFO rules.

## National Pension System (NPS)
NPS is a market-linked pension scheme regulated by PFRDA. Under the old tax regime an additional deduction of up to 50,000 rupees is available under Section 80CCD(1B) over the 80C limit. Employer contributions to NPS can be deductible under Section 80CCD(2) in both tax regimes within limits. Part of the corpus must be used to buy an annuity at retirement.
