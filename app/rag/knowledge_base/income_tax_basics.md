## Old versus new tax regime
Individual taxpayers can choose between the old regime, which allows many deductions such as 80C, 80D and home loan interest, and the new regime, which has lower slab rates but very few deductions. Salaried people can usually choose each year. The better regime depends on how many deductions you actually claim, so compare both.

## New regime slabs for FY 2025-26
For FY 2025-26 (assessment year 2026-27) the new regime slabs were: up to 4 lakh nil, 4 to 8 lakh 5 percent, 8 to 12 lakh 10 percent, 12 to 16 lakh 15 percent, 16 to 20 lakh 20 percent, 20 to 24 lakh 25 percent and above 24 lakh 30 percent. A rebate under Section 87A means no tax up to 12 lakh rupees of taxable income, and the standard deduction for salaried people is 75,000 rupees. Slabs change with each Union Budget, so confirm the figures for the current year.

## Old regime slabs
The old regime has a basic exemption of 2.5 lakh rupees, then 5 percent from 2.5 to 5 lakh, 20 percent from 5 to 10 lakh and 30 percent above 10 lakh, with a standard deduction of 50,000 rupees for salaried people. Health and education cess of 4 percent applies on top of the tax.

## Common deductions to save tax in the old regime
Section 80C allows up to 1.5 lakh rupees a year for investments such as PPF, EPF, ELSS mutual funds, NSC, life insurance premiums and home loan principal. Section 80D covers health insurance premiums. Section 24(b) covers home loan interest. Section 80CCD(1B) gives an extra 50,000 rupees for NPS.

## Filing basics
Most salaried taxpayers file an income tax return by 31 July of the assessment year, unless the government extends the date. Employers deduct tax at source (TDS) and Form 16 summarises it. Check Form 26AS or the Annual Information Statement for TDS credits before filing.
